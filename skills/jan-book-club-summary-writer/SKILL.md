# Jan Book Club Summary Writer

## What It Does

Drafts a structured recap of a fictional book club meeting, including the book title, key themes discussed, memorable quotes from participants, points of disagreement, and a closing takeaway.

## When To Use It

Use this skill when a learner wants to practice turning informal discussion notes into a polished, readable summary — without using any real personal or private context.

## Inputs

- The fictional book title and author.
- A short description of the discussion (bullet points or freeform notes are both fine).
- Any made-up participant names or quotes to include.
- The desired tone (casual, newsletter-style, formal recap, etc.).

## Output

A short summary (roughly 150–250 words) covering: the book, the main themes that came up, highlights from the discussion, any interesting disagreements, and a one-sentence takeaway the group landed on.

## Example Prompt

\```text
We just finished a fictional book called "The Cartographer's Lie" by a made-up author. Here are my rough notes from the discussion: everyone loved the ending, two people thought the main character was unreliable, and we debated whether the map was a metaphor. Can you write a polished book club recap?
\```

## Safety Notes

Use invented book titles, fictional authors, and made-up participant names only. Do not use real book club members, private discussion notes, real published works with extensive quotes, or any personal or workplace context.
