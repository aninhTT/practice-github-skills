# Jess Dog Park Meetup Planner

## What It Does

Turns a loose group chat about "we should take the dogs out sometime" into a concrete meetup plan. Picks a time and a park, then groups the dogs into play sessions by size and energy level so mismatched pairs are not turned loose together. Flags dogs that should stay leashed or sit out entirely.

## When To Use It

Use this skill when an invented group of dog owners wants a meetup plan and someone has to decide the boring parts: which park, what time, and which dogs can safely share a run. It is most useful when the group is large enough that a single free-for-all would go badly.

## Inputs

- A fictional list of dogs with name, rough size, and energy level (calm, medium, bouncy).
- Any made-up notes about temperament, such as "does not like puppies" or "guards the ball."
- The parks the invented group can reach, with fenced or unfenced noted.
- A rough window of days and times people are free.
- The made-up weather forecast for that window.

## Output

A short meetup plan containing:

- A recommended park and time, with one sentence on why.
- Two or three play groups, each listing which dogs are in it and roughly how long the session runs.
- A list of dogs to keep leashed, plus the reason.
- A small "bring this" checklist, such as water, spare bags, and a towel.
- One fallback option if the invented forecast turns bad.

## Example Prompt

```text
I have a fictional dog-park group with six dogs: a bouncy young lab, two
calm senior beagles, a tiny anxious terrier, a medium herding mix that
guards tennis balls, and a giant puppy with no manners yet. We can reach a
fenced park and an unfenced field. Everyone is free Saturday morning or
Sunday afternoon, and the made-up forecast says Saturday is windy and
Sunday is warm. Give me a meetup plan with safe play groups.
```

## Safety Notes

Use invented dogs, invented people, and invented parks only. Do not include real neighbors, real addresses, real park locations, home addresses, phone numbers, or anything that would identify a real person or their pet.

This skill produces a casual planning suggestion, not animal-behavior or veterinary advice. Real decisions about whether two dogs should meet belong to the owners and, where there is any doubt, to a qualified trainer or veterinarian.
