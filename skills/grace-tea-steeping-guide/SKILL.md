# Tea Steeping Guide

## What It Does

Takes whatever loose tea or bagged tea someone has on hand and turns it into a short brewing plan: water temperature, steep time, leaf-to-water ratio, and how many times the same leaves are worth re-steeping. It adjusts the plan for the gear actually available, so a mug and a kettle without a thermometer get different instructions than a gaiwan and a variable-temperature kettle.

## When To Use It

Use it when a tea tastes wrong and it is not obvious why — bitter green tea, thin oolong, a herbal blend that never seems strong enough. It is also useful when working through an unfamiliar tea, or when the only instruction on the package is "brew with hot water."

## Inputs

- The tea type or name, as written on the package (for example "sencha", "Earl Grey", "shou puerh", or "a green one from the market").
- How much tea is being brewed, in cups, milliliters, or "one big mug".
- The brewing gear: mug and infuser, teapot, French press, gaiwan, or just a bag in a cup.
- Whether the kettle can hit a specific temperature, or only boils.
- Taste preference: light, balanced, or strong.
- Optional: whether re-steeping matters, and whether the tea will be taken with milk.

## Output

- A first-steep spec: water temperature (with a no-thermometer substitute, such as "boil, then wait two minutes"), steep time, and a leaf-to-water ratio in grams and in spoons.
- A re-steep table, listing how many further steeps to expect and how much to extend each one.
- A short troubleshooting list mapping the likely off-flavors to the fix — bitter means cooler water or less time, flat means more leaf.
- One note on gear, where the chosen vessel changes the plan (a French press keeps leaves in the water, so it needs a shorter steep).

## Example Prompt

```text
I have a bag of loose-leaf sencha and a kettle that only boils. Brewing
one 350 ml mug with a basket infuser. Last time it came out really
bitter and a bit seaweedy. I like it on the lighter side. Give me a
plan, and tell me if these leaves are worth a second steep.
```

## Safety Notes

- This is a fictional skill written for GitHub practice. Do not feed it personal files, private notes, company or customer information, internal workflows, credentials, or anything copied from a real workplace document.
- Use invented or clearly public tea examples only. Do not paste brewing guides copied from a vendor's paid content.
- The skill covers flavor and brewing technique only. It gives no health, medical, caffeine-intake, or pregnancy advice, and it does not check herbal ingredients for interactions with medication.
- It assumes someone is handling near-boiling water and does not supervise that; usual kettle and hot-water care applies.
