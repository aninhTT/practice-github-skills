# Stephanie Christmas Movie Generator

## What It Does

Invents a brand-new, made-up Christmas movie from a few ingredients. It picks a cozy setting, a pair of leads, a festive problem to solve, and a heartwarming ending, then packages it all as a movie pitch with a punny title, a tagline, and a short scene-by-scene outline.

## When To Use It

Use this skill when someone wants a bit of holiday fun: a game for a holiday party, a writing prompt for a snowy afternoon, a "guess the cliché" bingo card, or just a laugh about how every holiday movie seems to follow the same recipe. It works best when the person gives it a few odd ingredients to combine, such as "a lighthouse, a rival bakery, and a lost reindeer."

## Inputs

- A setting, or a request for a random one (a snowed-in ski lodge, a tiny town with one stoplight, a big-city toy store).
- The type of lead characters (a busy city planner, a grumpy innkeeper, a retired elf).
- The festive problem (the town tree lighting is canceled, the family cookie recipe is missing, the holiday parade has no float).
- The tone: classic and sweet, extra cheesy, comedy, or mystery.
- Optional must-have clichés, such as a matching-sweater scene, a fake engagement, or a snowball fight.
- Optional length: a quick one-line pitch or a full outline.

## Output

- A punny movie title and a one-sentence tagline.
- A short cast list of fictional characters with a one-line description each.
- A three-act outline: the arrival, the festive complication, and the big holiday finale.
- A "cliché count" listing every holiday movie trope the plot uses.
- An optional runner-up title, in case the first one is not cheesy enough.

## Example Prompt

```text
Make me a Christmas movie. Setting: a tiny mountain town famous for its
gingerbread festival. Leads: a stressed-out architect sent to tear down the
old candy shop, and the candy shop's cheerful owner. Problem: the festival's
giant gingerbread house collapsed three days before Christmas. Make it extra
cheesy, include a snowball fight, and give me a punny title.
```

## Safety Notes

This is a fictional practice skill. All movies, characters, towns, and businesses are invented. Do not include personal files, private notes, real people, company or customer information, internal workflows, credentials, or anything copied from a real workplace document. Do not copy plots, characters, or dialogue from real movies; every pitch should be original and made up.
