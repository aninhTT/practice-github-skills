# Nathan Potluck Dish Coordinator

## What It Does

Helps plan a fictional potluck by tracking who is bringing which dish, grouping the dishes into categories (appetizers, mains, sides, desserts, drinks), and flagging gaps or overlaps so the imaginary spread stays balanced.

## When To Use It

Use this skill when a learner wants to practice organizing a made-up group meal — for example, coordinating dishes for a pretend office party or a fictional neighborhood get-together — without using any real names or private event details.

## Inputs

- A fictional list of guests and the dish each one plans to bring.
- The number of expected guests.
- Any dish categories the learner wants to balance (for example: appetizers, mains, sides, desserts, drinks).
- Optional notes, such as invented dietary preferences to account for.

## Output

A grouped dish list organized by category, a count of dishes per category, a short list of gaps to fill (for example, "no desserts yet"), and a flag for any duplicate dishes.

## Example Prompt

```text
Here is a fake potluck for 10 pretend guests. Sam is bringing chips, Jordan is bringing a salad, and two guests both signed up for pasta. Help me balance the categories and tell me what's missing.
```

## Safety Notes

Use invented guests and events only. Do not use real event details, actual guest lists, coworker or customer names, home addresses, contact information, or any private or company data.
