# Hanna Pottery Glaze Combo Picker

## What It Does

Takes a list of made-up glazes sitting on a studio shelf and suggests layering combinations worth trying on a specific piece. For each combo it explains where to dip, where to leave bare clay, and which pairs are likely to run down onto the kiln shelf. It also proposes a small test-tile order so the risky combos get proven on a scrap piece before anyone commits a finished pot to them.

## When To Use It

Use this skill when someone has a piece ready to glaze, a shelf of half-used buckets, and no idea which ones play well together. It is most useful when the glazes have never been layered before, when a piece has an awkward shape like a wide flare or a narrow foot that makes runs more likely, or when there is only one shot at a piece and guessing feels expensive.

## Inputs

- The clay body, in whatever terms the potter uses ("the speckled brown stoneware", "porcelain").
- The glazes on hand, with names and a rough description of each: color, and whether it is glossy, satin, or matte.
- Target firing temperature or cone.
- The shape of the piece, including any flared rims, sharp corners, or narrow feet.
- How the piece will be used, such as a mug for daily drinking or a planter for a windowsill.
- Any past results the potter remembers, including combos that ran or went muddy.
- How much appetite there is for risk on this particular piece.

## Output

- Three to five layering combos, ranked from safest to most experimental.
- For each combo: the dip order, roughly how far up the piece each layer should come, and the look to expect.
- A run-risk flag on any combo where a glaze is likely to move in the firing, with a suggested bare margin above the foot.
- A short test-tile plan covering the riskiest pairs, so they get tried on scrap first.
- Notes on which combos suit the stated use, including which to keep off a drinking surface.

## Example Prompt

```text
I have a flared 7-inch bowl in speckled brown stoneware, firing to cone 6.
On the shelf I have Harbor Blue (glossy), Oatmeal (satin), and Rust Break
(matte, and it ran on me last time). The bowl is for serving salad. The
rim flares out a lot. Suggest some layering combos, tell me which ones are
likely to run, and give me a test tile plan before I risk the bowl.
```

## Safety Notes

This is a fictional practice skill. The glazes, studio, and past results are all invented, and the suggestions are creative guesses rather than tested ceramic chemistry. Do not include personal files, private notes, company or customer information, internal workflows, credentials, or anything copied from a real workplace document. Real glazing involves kiln heat and materials that can be hazardous raw, so treat any actual firing or food-surface decision as something to confirm with a studio technician and the real manufacturer's data, not with this skill.
